Twelve questions that tell you whether a fractional CMO builds or just advises

By Logan Ice · Last updated October 6, 2026

Plenty of fractional marketing leaders are strategy-only, and most interview guides won't catch that until month three. These are the questions I'd ask if I were hiring someone like me, including the two that most candidates can't answer: show me the last ad account you personally ran, and how do you plan to make yourself unnecessary.

Why the usual interview misses

The standard "questions to ask a fractional CMO" lists are about strategy, culture and communication. All fine. None of them catch the thing that actually goes wrong: you hire a senior person expecting them to fix growth, and it turns out they direct other people to fix growth. If you don't have those other people, you've bought a plan and no hands.

So the questions below are weighted toward finding out whether this person does work, whether they'll be honest with you, and what you'll be left with when they're gone. Ask me these too. I've put my own answers at the end so you can compare.

The twelve questions

  1. Show me the last ad account you personally ran in the past 90 days. Not oversaw. Ran. If the answer is a pause, you're talking to an adviser, which is fine as long as you know it.
  2. How would you connect our ad spend to contribution margin? You want to hear "MER against a break-even derived from your margin," or something equivalent. If you hear "ROAS" as the top-line metric, keep going.
  3. What will my team be able to do after you leave that they can't do today? The only good answer is specific: run the creative testing loop, read the dashboard, launch and grade a campaign, hire the next person.
  4. How do you plan to make yourself unnecessary? Nobody sells against their own retainer easily. The ones who have a real answer are the ones you want.
  5. What's the last thing you told a CEO they were wrong about, and what happened? You're hiring judgment. Judgment without spine is decoration.
  6. Walk me through your first 30 days here, specifically. Vague equals inexperienced. You should hear an audit, a scorecard, a 90-day list of bets, and a first set of tests.
  7. Which of our channels would you turn off first, and how would you know you were right? Tests the incrementality instinct and whether they'll spend your money carefully.
  8. What do you not do? Everyone has a gap. Someone who claims brand, performance, creative, lifecycle, data and PR at senior level is describing a department, not a person.
  9. Who else are you working with right now, and how fast will we hear back from you? Fractional means shared. You want to know how many clients they're carrying and what a normal reply time looks like, and you want the answers to add up. Same day is a reasonable bar; "I'll get to it next week" isn't ownership.
  10. What happens to the systems, logins and documentation when we part ways? Should be: they're yours, in your accounts, in your name, from day one.
  11. How do you use AI in the work? Not as a buzzword. Specifically: creative production, reporting, testing, research. If the answer is "we're exploring it," they're behind.
  12. What would make you turn this engagement down? A good operator has a list. Under a certain revenue, thin margins, a founder who wants a button-pusher.

What the numbers say

  • CEOs and marketing leaders grade the job differently. In McKinsey's 2025 survey of Fortune 1000 C-suites, 70% of CEOs said they measure marketing on year-over-year revenue growth and margin, but only 35% of CMOs tracked that as a top metric. (McKinsey, The CMO's comeback, June 2025)
  • Most marketing chiefs are doing it for the first time. Spencer Stuart found 68% of Fortune 500 CMOs in 2024 were first-time CMOs, which is a reason to ask for recent hands-on work rather than assume seniority means experience. (Spencer Stuart, March 2025)
  • Fractional means shared, so ask for the number. The Fractional Work Report 2026 found 64% of fractional workers serve two or more clients, and the median bills 21 client-facing hours a week across them. (Fractional Jobs, 2026)
  • The hands question is worth asking either way. In that same report, 91% of fractional workers say they do hands-on execution, not just advice, which is also what every candidate will tell you; question one is how you check. (Fractional Jobs, 2026)

A CMO on the metric question

I don't think people spend enough time aligning on the right metrics.

Norm de Greve, Senior Vice President and Chief Marketing Officer, General Motors, in McKinsey's The CMO's comeback

Question two exists because of that. If you and the candidate don't agree on the number before the engagement starts, you will argue about it every month after.

Red flags I'd act on

A percentage-of-spend fee structure for a "leader" role. Case studies with no numbers or no names. A first-30-days plan that's all discovery and no tests. Any reluctance to be measured on the blended number. A team appearing behind them that you didn't know you were hiring.

Green flags

They ask about your contribution margin before they ask about your ad spend. They have opinions you disagree with. They name what they won't do. They can show you a dashboard they built and explain what a client did with it on a Tuesday.

Questions people ask

What questions should I ask a fractional CMO before hiring?

Ask to see the last ad account they personally ran, how they'd connect spend to contribution margin, what your team will be able to do after they leave, how they plan to make themselves unnecessary, and when they last told a CEO they were wrong.

How do I know if my Meta ads agency is doing a good job?

Grade them on blended MER against a target derived from your contribution margin, not on the ROAS in their own report. If MER is at or above target and creative is being tested every week, they're doing fine.

What metrics should a fractional growth lead be accountable for?

Blended MER, contribution dollars from marketing, new-customer growth and retention or repeat rate. Platform ROAS and CPA are diagnostics, not grades.

Should a fractional CMO have their own team?

Sometimes, if you know that's what you're buying and the fee reflects it. Be wary when a "leader" hire quietly turns into an agency engagement. Ask who does the work and where they sit.

What's the difference between a fractional CMO and a marketing consultant?

A consultant hands you recommendations. A fractional CMO takes ownership of the function on a part-time basis. An embedded fractional growth lead, which is how I work, owns the number and also does a lot of the work.
