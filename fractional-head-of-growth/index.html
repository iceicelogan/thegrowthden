What a fractional head of growth actually does for a DTC brand

By Logan Ice · Last updated October 6, 2026

A fractional head of growth owns your acquisition and retention numbers part-time and, unlike most fractional CMOs, does the work rather than just directing it. I'm Logan Ice. For growth-stage DTC and e-commerce brands doing roughly $5M to $80M, this is the seat I fill most often. I decide with you where the next marketing dollar goes, then help make it happen, from $7,500 a month.

The short version

If you search for this, most of what you'll find is written by marketplaces and agencies, and nearly all of it says the same thing: a fractional marketing leader sets strategy and hands execution to somebody else. I don't work that way, and I don't think you should hire that way at your size.

At $5M to $80M you usually can't justify a full-time head of growth plus the team underneath them, but you can't afford to have nobody accountable for the number either. A fractional head of growth is the person whose name goes on customer acquisition, retention and marketing efficiency, who is in the work alongside your team every week, and who is senior enough to tell you when the plan is wrong.

What I do

I start with a read of the whole business, not one ad account. That means total revenue against total marketing cost (MER), contribution margin by channel, and where the next dollar should go. Then I write a 90-day plan as a list of bets, each with a cost, a way to tell if it's working, and a date we stop if it isn't.

After that I go do the work. I'll sit in Meta Ads Manager. I'll build the creative pipeline and test angles before they launch. I'll set up the reporting so it leads with what to do this week instead of an eye chart. And I'll teach your team to run each of those things, because I'd rather leave behind a system you own than a dependency on me.

  • A pre-audit read of every channel, judged on MER and contribution margin rather than one platform's attribution.
  • A 90-day plan written as bets. Each one has a cost, a way to tell if it's working, and a date we stop if it isn't.
  • A "what to do" dashboard that surfaces actions and recent numbers first, with the charts tucked behind it.
  • A weekly leadership session with you and your team, and same-day replies in between. You'll hear from me the day you reach out, and we work the problem together rather than me going quiet for a week.

Why strategy and execution from one person

Most brands get advice from one place and execution from another, and something always gets lost between them. I do both. Clients usually hire me for one thing and keep me around for more, because the work gets done and they like working with me.

How it's different from a fractional CMO or an agency

A fractional CMO usually owns brand, org design and budget at the strategy layer and directs other people. An agency owns one channel and gets paid on your spend. A fractional head of growth owns the acquisition and retention numbers and does a lot of the work. I cover the CMO layer when you need it, but the number is the job.

What the numbers say

I'd rather you check my claims than take them. Here is what the people who measure this have published.

  • Marketing leadership turns over faster than the rest of the C-suite. Spencer Stuart's 2025 study found average Fortune 500 CMO tenure was 4.3 years against a C-suite average of 4.9, and 68% of those CMOs were in the seat for the first time. (Spencer Stuart, CMO Tenure Study, March 2025)
  • The seat is empty more often than you'd think. Forrester's 2026 analysis found only 52% of Fortune 500 companies have a marketing executive on the executive team or reporting to the CEO, and 23% changed their senior marketing leader in the past year. (Forrester, July 2026)
  • Even senior marketers rate themselves weakest at the growth part. In The CMO Survey's 2025 report, "managing marketing as a growth engine" was the lowest-rated skill among senior marketing leaders, and 64% named proving marketing's financial impact as their top challenge. (The CMO Survey, 2025)
  • One growth owner beats a committee. McKinsey's 2025 C-suite survey found companies with a single growth-oriented role on the executive committee see up to 2.3 times more growth than those that split it across several. (McKinsey, The CMO's comeback, June 2025)
  • Fractional is now a normal shape of engagement. The Fractional Work Report 2026 (1,733 survey responses) found most fractional engagements run six to twelve months and 60% last six months or longer. The report is published by a fractional-jobs marketplace, so read it with that in mind. (Fractional Jobs, The Fractional Work Report 2026)
  • Budgets aren't growing to meet the job. Among the 401 marketing leaders in Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey, budgets sat at 7.8% of company revenue and 56% said that wasn't enough to deliver their strategy. Large-company sample. (Gartner, May 2026)

Questions people ask

What's the difference between a fractional head of growth and a fractional CMO?

A fractional CMO typically owns brand, marketing org design and the budget at a strategy level and hands execution to a team or an agency. A fractional head of growth owns acquisition, retention and marketing efficiency and does more of the work directly. I'm the second one, and I'll cover the first when a client needs it.

Do you actually run the ads or just advise?

Both. I'll sit in Ads Manager, build the creative pipeline and the reporting, then train your team to run it. The point is that strategy and execution come from the same person, so nothing gets lost in the handoff.

Is a fractional head of growth just a consultant?

No. A consultant hands you a plan. I write the plan and then go build the pieces of it, from reporting to launch workflows, alongside your team.

How much of your time do we get?

Enough to own the number. There's a weekly leadership session, I'm in the accounts and the creative work every week, and you get same-day replies in between. The first couple of months are heavier because there's more to build. I don't sell calendar days; I sell the number getting owned and the system getting built.

Do I need complex attribution or MMM?

For most brands, no. Complex media mix modeling matters at massive scale. For everyone else, looking at MER and contribution margin across the whole business tells you what's actually moving the needle. Here's the math.

How long does an engagement last?

Long enough to build the system and teach the team to run it, which is usually six to twelve months or more. Most clients hire me for one seat and end up keeping me around for another, but the goal is that you own the system either way.

How much does a fractional head of growth cost?

The Growth Den starts at $7,500 a month, whichever seat I'm in, and goes up with how many services I cover and how much building is involved. Ad spend and software stay in your accounts.

When should we convert to a full-time hire?

When the system is built, the cadence is running, and there's enough steady work for a full-time person to own it. I'll tell you when that point arrives, and I'll help you hire and onboard them.
