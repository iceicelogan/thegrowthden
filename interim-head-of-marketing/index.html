An interim head of marketing, or CMO, for the gap between leaders

By Logan Ice · Last updated October 6, 2026

When your marketing leader leaves, goes on leave, or hasn't been hired yet, the team and the budget still need an owner on Monday. I step into the seat for a fixed window, usually three to six months, run the team and the number, and help you hire and hand over to the permanent person. I'm Logan Ice. Like every service here, it starts at $7,500 a month and is scoped to the size of the job.

When this is the right service

  • Your head of marketing or CMO just left, and a proper search takes longer than the plan can sit still.
  • Someone senior is going on leave and the plan can't sit still for four months.
  • You raised money or went into retail and need a real marketing leader before you're ready to hire one for good.
  • You've never had a marketing leader, the founder has been doing it, and it's time to stop.

What I do

  • Own the marketing team, the budget and the blended number, and run the weekly cadence that keeps everyone pointed at it.
  • Grade every agency and in-house buyer on MER and contribution margin, not their own platform's report.
  • Keep the creative pipeline, the reporting and the launches moving. This is not a caretaker role. Growth still has to happen.
  • Write the job the permanent leader will actually do, help you hire them, and onboard them into a system that already works.

How it's set up

A window we agree on up front, usually three to six months, revisited monthly. This is the whole marketing function rather than one part of it, so it's scoped to the size of your team and budget. You get the weekly cadence, me in the work every week, and same-day replies: you'll hear from me the day you reach out, and we solve things together rather than you waiting on a status update. There's no hard exit either. If you'd like me to stay on in a strategy or creative seat once your permanent hire lands, that happens more often than not.

What the numbers say

  • Marketing leadership turns over often. Spencer Stuart's 2025 study put average Fortune 500 CMO tenure at 4.3 years, and found 34% of the Fortune 500 had no enterprise CMO at all; Forrester's 2026 analysis found 23% of Fortune 500 companies changed their senior marketing leader in the past year. (Spencer Stuart, March 2025; Forrester, July 2026)
  • Interim leadership has become a normal way to cover it. Heidrick & Struggles' 2026 independent talent report found demand for interim C-suite leaders up 151% since 2021, with interim CMO requests doubling year over year; its 2025 analysis counted a 310% rise in US interim executive placements since 2020. One placement firm's data. (Heidrick & Struggles / BTG, 2026 report; Heidrick & Struggles, July 2025)
  • Smaller companies are most of that demand, and the windows are longer than people assume. Heidrick's 2026 survey of 3,810 interim leaders found small and medium companies make up more than four-fifths of demand, 42% of projects now run longer than six months, and 75% of companies hire interims to fill a critical skill gap. (Heidrick & Struggles, 2026 Talent Lens survey)
  • The search itself takes a while. SHRM's 2025 benchmarking puts median time-to-fill for executive roles at about 45 days, and retained search firms lay out roughly 90 days from kickoff to a signed offer, before notice periods and onboarding. (SHRM, October 2025; Hunt Scanlon / The Bowdoin Group, 2022)

A search firm's view of the interim seat

Interim leaders are no longer a stopgap; they are how forward-looking organizations turn volatility into advantage—bringing the precise expertise, speed, and objectivity needed to move critical agendas forward, exactly when and where it matters most.

Sunny Ackerman, Global Managing Partner, Heidrick & Struggles, in the 2026 Talent Lens survey

How we start

  1. Call me the week the gap opens, or before it does if you can see it coming. The first conversation is about the team, the budget and the number, not the org chart.
  2. Agree the window and the scope up front, with a monthly check on both.
  3. In the first two weeks: a read of every channel, the scorecard on MER against your margin, and the team's weekly cadence running.
  4. By month two I know what the permanent job actually is. The description, the interview rubric and the search plan come from that.
  5. Hand over into a system that already works, and decide together whether I stay in a smaller seat.

Questions people ask

How is this different from your other services?

The other services are one part of marketing, for as long as it's useful. This one is the whole function, for a defined window with an end you can see. I run the team, the budget and the number, then hand the seat to whoever comes next.

How long does an interim engagement last?

Usually three to six months, and sometimes longer; Heidrick's 2026 survey found 42% of interim projects now run past six months. We agree the window up front and revisit it monthly. If you want me to stay on in a smaller seat after, plenty of clients do that.

Will you help hire my permanent head of marketing?

Yes, and I'd argue it's the most valuable part. By month two I know exactly what the job is, so I can write the description, sit in the interviews, and onboard the person into a system that's already running instead of a mess I left behind.

What does the interim service cost?

From $7,500 a month, like every service here, scoped up from there to the size of the team and the budget I'm running. Ad spend and software stay in your accounts, as always.
